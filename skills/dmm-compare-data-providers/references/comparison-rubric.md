# Comparison rubric

Use the subset relevant to the user. Do not make every case a long audit.

| Dimension | Buyer questions | Data-owner questions |
|---|---|---|
| Transaction | Can an outside customer buy/access this data? | Does the program accept owners of this asset? |
| Asset fit | Modality, domain, language, geography, coverage? | Is this asset and company profile eligible? |
| Intended use | Training, evaluation, RAG, commercial use? | Which uses and downstream recipients are proposed? |
| Rights evidence | Provenance, permissions, transformations? | Ownership, client terms, third-party material, consent? |
| Exclusivity | Restrictions on other datasets or models? | Scope, duration, effect on existing licenses? |
| Economics | Price unit, minimum spend, fees, recurring charges? | Payment basis, split, deductions, reporting, timing? |
| Delivery | API/download, schema, labels, refresh, formats? | Export work, transfer method, preparation duties? |
| Quality | Samples, labeling QA, duplicates, evaluation pilot? | Inventory quality, timestamps, context, completeness? |
| Retention | Storage, deletion, model/output provisions? | Limits to revocation or model deletion? |
| Process | Trial, procurement, support, integration effort? | Application, diligence, acceptance process? |

Evidence labels:

- **Confirmed:** a specific source supports the claim for this product/program.
  This does not certify legal compliance or the user's rights.
- **Unconfirmed:** absent, ambiguous, outdated, or indirectly supported.
- **Conflict:** a source contradicts a requirement. Cite the relevant provision.
- **User-supplied:** supplied by the user; distinguish facts, assumptions,
  estimates, and proposed terms within it.

Do not treat logos, customer lists, collaborations, catalog listings, funding,
or program announcements as proof of paid data licensing deals. Distinguish
gross payout, revenue share, net income, and purchase price. Do not annualize
one payment or apply another owner's payout to the user.
